Section 3The whole system at a glance

One system, reached three different ways. Same data, same accounts, same reports — whichever one you open.

Why one server decides everything

In your current system, every computer connects straight to the database. That is fast, but it means each computer decides for itself what is allowed.

In the new system there is a server in the middle. Every invoice, payment and stock movement passes through it, and it checks the rules — is the period closed, is the customer over his credit limit, does the entry balance — before anything is written. This is what makes the system safe to sell to other businesses, because a customer cannot break their own books by accident.

Section 4One ledger at the centre

This is the most important idea in the whole system, and everything else follows from it.

Accounting is not a module that sits beside stock and sales. It is the floor that everything stands on. When you sell something, the sale writes a debit and a credit. When stock leaves the warehouse, that writes a debit and a credit. When you run payroll, when you depreciate an asset, when a cheque bounces — every one of them writes into the same ledger.

Three rules we will not break

Rule one

Posted is permanent

While a document is a draft you can change it freely. Once it is posted it can never be edited or deleted — only reversed by a new entry. So last month's reports stay the same forever.

Rule two

The moment is frozen

The exchange rate, the price, the tax rate, the box size — whatever was true when you posted stays on the document. Change them tomorrow and history does not move.

Rule three

Every number explains itself

Click any figure on any report and you see the entries behind it, and who posted them, and when. Nothing in the system is a number without a story.

A small thing that matters a lot

We store money as exact decimal numbers, not as the “floating point” numbers most small systems use. At 90,000 pounds to the dollar, that difference is real: floating point loses tiny fractions on every calculation, and after a year those fractions are why a trial balance is out by a few pounds that nobody can find. Ours cannot do that.

Section 5Dollars and pounds

You told us the heart of the problem: your customers deal in dollars, the government wants pounds. Here is exactly how we handle it.

Every entry holds three amounts

When you raise an invoice in dollars, we do not convert it and throw the dollars away, and we do not keep two separate sets of books. We store all three numbers on the same line.

Something extra we are giving you

We also store the US dollar value on every single entry, the same way. That means you get a full balance sheet and profit and loss in dollars and in pounds — and neither one is a translation of the other, so they can never disagree. For a business where 90% of trade is in dollars but the state only accepts pounds, this is worth a lot.

Two rates, and more when you need them

Your system today keeps one rate for each currency. You told us you actually use two — a sale rate of 90,000 and a purchase rate of 89,500. So the new system keeps a rate list, and each rate has a type and a date:

Rate typeUsed forExample
SaleEvery sales invoice and customer receipt90,000

Rates are never overwritten. When the official rate changes you add a new rate with a new date. Everything posted before keeps the old one. And because entering a rate is one of the most powerful actions in the system, only named people can do it, the screen shows you the old rate and the percentage change before you confirm, and every change is recorded with who and when.

The difference of exchange report — both halves

You asked for this by name. There are two halves, and most small systems only build the first.

Half one · realised

The customer paid at a different rate

You invoiced at 90,000. He pays two months later when the rate is 91,000. The difference is real money and it posts to your exchange account the moment the payment is matched.

And because you told us customers pay in instalments — “yes in business all is acceptable” — each payment takes its own rate. Three payments across three months means three separate differences, each at the rate of its own day. Not one calculation at the end.

Half two · unrealised

The rate moved and nobody has paid yet

At the end of each month, every unpaid dollar invoice, every dollar supplier balance and every dollar bank account is worth a different number of pounds than it was. That difference has to be posted or the balance sheet is wrong that morning.

This is the half usually left out, and with 90% of your invoices in dollars it is the half that matters most to you.

The report shows both, for every customer and every supplier: the original amount, the rate on the document, the rate at payment or at month end, the difference in pounds, and which account it went to.

One detail your accountant will check

Only money accounts get revalued — cash, banks, customers, suppliers. Stock and fixed assets do not, because they are not money and international accounting rules say they stay at their original cost. Most home-made systems revalue everything, which is wrong. Each account in your chart carries a setting saying which treatment it gets, and your accountant confirms it once at the start.

Why the “Used by” tab exists

The most expensive mistake in an accounting system is quietly changing something that other things depend on. Before you block an account, this tab tells you that five posting rules and a hundred and seventy-six customers point at it. Then it tells you plainly what the system will refuse: you cannot delete an account with movements, you can move it under another parent, and you can merge it — each with a note on what happens to the history.

Two decisions we have taken for you

Balances are never stored on the account

Your current system keeps a running debit and credit on each account row, and a second table of balances beside the journal. We are not carrying either across. Every figure is worked out from the entries themselves, with a fast summary kept for speed and checked against the real entries every night. A stored balance sitting next to a journal is the classic way a chart of accounts quietly stops agreeing with itself, and nobody notices for four months.

The account numbers are a starting point, not a decision

We load a full Lebanese chart — your seven classes, five levels deep, around a hundred and fifty accounts, in both languages. Your accountant confirms the exact numbers in the first week. Changing one costs nothing, because no account number is written inside the programme — only in the linked accounts screen. That is the single biggest improvement over what you have now, where the numbers are built into the software itself.

Customer classification New

What you asked for

“Customer setup and classification — cash only, maximum discount, minimum discount, credit limit.”

How it works

You create groups — for example Cash only, Wholesale, Key account — and each group carries its own rules: which price level it gets by default, the smallest and largest discount a salesman may give, whether credit is allowed at all, and the default payment terms.

Then every customer belongs to a group and inherits those rules. A salesman who tries to go outside the discount band gets stopped, and a manager can override it with a reason that is recorded.

Payment terms — you create them New

What you asked for

“Payment term will be dynamic, so if a company has a special payment term it is possible to create it and link it to an account on the chart of accounts.”

How it works

You create as many terms as you need — cash on delivery, cheque on delivery, bank transfer, net 30, net 90, three instalments, or a special arrangement used by one customer only. Nothing is a fixed list built into the software.

Each term says how it behaves — paid on the spot, one payment after so many days, end of month, or split into instalments with your own percentages and dates — and each term names the account it uses.

Why linking to an account matters

This is the part that is a real accounting decision rather than a setting, and it is worth being clear about.

  • A term paid on the spot names where the money lands — the cash box, the bank, or the cheques account. No customer balance is created at all
  • A term that gives credit names which customer control account carries the balance. Most sales go to the normal customer account — but you can send instalment or long-dated sales to a separate one

The reason to do that: your balance sheet then separates money due soon from money due later, instead of showing one figure for everything owed to you. Your accountant will recognise this immediately.

And the protection that comes with it

When money owed to you sits in more than one account, each account is reconciled against its own open invoices — so both still agree with the ledger exactly. The account used is written onto each invoice when it posts, so changing a term next year never re-classifies what happened last year.

The system also checks that the account you pick is the right kind. A customer balance can only point at a customer control account; a cash term can only point at cash, bank or cheques. It will not let a wrong link be saved.

Balances, ageing and instalments Improved

How instalments are handled

When an invoice has a payment plan, the system does not keep one balance and count down. It creates one open item for each instalment, each with its own due date.

That one decision makes everything else work correctly: the ageing report is truthful, the reminder knows which instalment is late, and — most importantly — each instalment calculates its own exchange difference at the rate of the day it was paid.

Ageing

The buckets are yours to set — 30, 60, 90 days or whatever you use — and you can run the report by due date (for collection) or by document date (for provisions). Both views, same data.

Credit control

What a customer “owes” is more than his invoice balance. The system counts posted invoices, goods delivered but not yet invoiced, and open orders, minus any money on account. That total is what is checked against his limit.

Where your customers are

District, region, city — kept, because you already use them

Your current system stores a district, a region and a city on every customer, and your invoices carry them. All three come across, as one tree you maintain yourself, with a salesman attached to each area.

It is not decoration. It is what lets you see sales by area, give a route to a salesman, plan a van's day, and set a delivery charge per zone in the restaurant. Sales by area was one of the first things you asked to be able to see.

Section 8Cheques, cash boxes and banks

You told us cheque collection on due date is normal practice in Lebanon. So we treat a cheque properly — as a thing with a life of its own, not as a word on a payment.

Most small systems record “paid by cheque” and stop there. That is not how the business actually works. A post-dated cheque sits in your drawer for two months, it might go to a supplier instead of the bank, and if it bounces it has to unwind everything and reopen the customer's balance.

You receive a customer's chequeThe customer's balance is cleared. The cheque becomes an asset in its own account — not cash, because it is not cash yet

Section 11What your stock is worth

You asked for three methods, selectable: last price, average, and FIFO. Here is what each one means in plain terms.

Method one

Last price

Everything is valued at the most recent price you paid. Simplest to explain. This is closest to how your system works today.

Method two

Average

Every purchase blends into a running average cost. Steady, and the least surprising when prices move up and down.

Method three

FIFO

First in, first out. The oldest stock is sold first, at the price you actually paid for that batch. The most accurate for profit, and the most work to keep.

The method is chosen per item, with a default for the company — so a fast commodity can be on average while a controlled item is on FIFO. Changing an item's method applies from a date forward; it never rewrites what happened before.

Why this is a real change

Today, when you enter a purchase, the new cost is written over the item's old cost. That means the value of what you bought last month quietly changes when you buy the same thing cheaper this month. In the new system every receipt keeps its own cost, so the value of your stock and the profit on every sale are calculated from what you actually paid.

Landed cost New

Freight, customs duty and clearing charges can be spread over a shipment — by value, weight or quantity — so they become part of what the goods cost. For an importer this is the difference between knowing your margin and guessing it.
